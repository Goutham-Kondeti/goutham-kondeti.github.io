Overview

Maintaining reliable global safety intake during major system and workflow change.

This sanitized industry case study summarizes work performed across Oracle Argus Safety and LSMV in a large global pharmacovigilance environment. It combines high-volume intake and triage, database migration, E2B and email queue support, reconciliation, incident management, UAT, automation, mentoring, quality control and collaboration with business and technical teams.

System migration

Supporting transition from Argus Safety to LSMV.

Learn and translate the new workflow

Understood how source documents, email intake and E2B messages moved through the new platform after DIMS was removed from the process.

Train and quality-review users

Mentored 10 team members during migration and reviewed their work while continuing duplicate correction, triage and urgent case support.

Manage queue failures

Investigated read, failed and processing queues; manually created cases or linked documents when required; identified patterns and escalated issues to IT and the client.

Support reconciliation teams

Explained how to search the safety database, email intake and E2B queues, rather than only supplying case numbers.

Protect reporting timelines

Prioritized death and life-threatening SUSARs, monitored urgent work and supported cutover and post-migration backlogs.

Automation and process improvement

Reducing manual work without weakening control.

Configurable line-listing splitter

Vendor spreadsheets could contain more than 100 patient records. The consolidated VBA design replaced hard-coded ranges and patient-identifier columns with interactive selections and supported either separate workbooks or multiple worksheets.

Result: applicable preparation was reduced from almost a full working day to approximately five minutes.

Incident management

Functional investigation connected business impact to technical action.

Issues were not treated as isolated system errors. I analyzed what failed, which cases were affected, the reporting risk, the likely pattern and the immediate operational workaround. I then coordinated with IT, client, E2B, reconciliation, safety review, source-document intake and quality teams.

UAT and quality

Validating changes in the context of the actual workflow.

  • Performed UAT for fixes and enhancements in sandbox and validation environments.
  • Validated whether changes supported intake, routing, queue behavior, document handling and business requirements.
  • Prepared or followed scenario-based tests, documented defects and supported retesting.
  • Created CAPA-focused presentations for duplicate errors and process learning.
  • Wrote MICC SOP documentation and helped standardize team practices.
  • Supported client and third-party audits by explaining processes, evidence and controls.

Key lessons

What this experience demonstrates.

  • Safety-system support requires both regulatory workflow knowledge and technical curiosity.
  • A workaround is only acceptable when traceability, data integrity and reporting timelines remain protected.
  • Automation should be resilient to source variation and must include a controlled fallback.
  • Migration success depends on user training, reconciliation, queue monitoring and post-cutover support—not only data transfer.
  • Clear dashboards and impact statements help technical teams prioritize safety-critical issues.
